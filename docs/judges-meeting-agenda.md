# Judges' Pre-Judging Alignment Meeting — Agenda

> **Purpose:** Align all judges on evaluation criteria, scoring methodology, and judging procedures **before** judging begins, so that scores are consistent, comparable, and defensible.

---

## 1. Meeting Title

**Judges' Pre-Judging Alignment Meeting**

This title intentionally reflects the true purpose: aligning judges on criteria, scoring, and procedures prior to the start of judging (not merely before submissions are due).

---

## 2. Team Status & Eligibility Categories

Teams are grouped into **three mutually exclusive** status categories. A team must belong to exactly one category; the categories must **not overlap**.

| Old Term | Revised Term | Notes |
|----------|--------------|-------|
| Not Competition | **Not Participating / Not Eligible** | Clarified scope |
| Out of Contact | **Unable to Contact** | Clarified scope |
| Fees Received Teams | **Teams with Fees Paid** | Clarified scope |

**Categories:**

1. **Not Participating / Not Eligible** — team withdrew, was disqualified, or does not meet eligibility rules.
2. **Unable to Contact** — registration exists but the team cannot be reached for confirmation/submission.
3. **Teams with Fees Paid** — eligible, confirmed, and cleared to be judged.

> ⚠️ **Action required:** The organizing committee must confirm that these three categories are mutually exclusive (no team appears in more than one). If the available data could place a team in two categories, stop and ask for clarification rather than assigning a category silently.

## 4. Problem Relevance Assessment

**Problem Relevance & Validation** is included as a **standalone** evaluation criterion (row 1 above). Judges must explicitly assess whether the problem is:

- **Real** — grounded in an actual need or pain point, not hypothetical;
- **Significant** — material in scale or importance for the target users/community;
- **Validated** — supported by evidence (user research, data, stakeholder input, or a documented validation method).

This keeps problem relevance distinct from outcome-based impact, while Social Impact (row 4) measures the expected or demonstrated effect of the solution.

## 5. Judging Procedures & Rules

The following rules ensure the process is transparent, fair, and consistent across all judges.

1. **Individual vs. consensus scoring**
   - _TBD:_ State whether judges score **independently** (scores aggregated/ averaged) or meet to reach **consensus**. Define how independent scores are combined (e.g., average, median, drop-high-low).

2. **Conflict-of-interest declaration & recusal**
   - Each judge declares any relationship (family, school, mentorship, commercial) to a team **before** scoring.
   - Conflicted judges **recuse** from scoring that team; the recusal and reason are logged.

3. **Incomplete or late submissions**
   - Define how partial submissions are scored (penalized, or scored only on submitted deliverables).
   - Define the late-submission policy (accepted with penalty, or disqualified).

4. **Prototype / demo requirements**
   - State the **minimum** expectation: e.g., a working prototype, or a **live or recorded demo** of the solution.
   - Define what constitutes an acceptable recorded demo (length, content, originality checks).

5. **Finalist selection**
   - Define the shortlisting method: e.g., top-N by weighted total, or threshold cutoff, or panel review of the top band.

6. **Tie-breaking**
   - Define a deterministic method for resolving tied weighted totals (e.g., higher score on Problem Relevance & Validation, then Innovation, then judge panel vote).

> All six procedural topics above must be present and approved before judging starts. Items marked **TBD** require organizer confirmation.
